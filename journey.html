The Journey

Where a project goes, stage by stage

← Deadlines

This is not a ranking of venues. It is the path one project takes to finish: an idea gets aired at a workshop, the real result lands as a full paper with a poster, and the mature version is archived in a journal. Most work wants all three. The only place grades apply is the middle stage, where the same finished paper could go to two different calibres of venue.

Stage 1 Rabbit Hole

Where you jump in with a half-formed idea. Workshops: five pages, a position, and an argument you are not sure of yet. Low stakes by design, fast turnaround, and the room that tells you whether the idea survives contact with people. Skipping this stage is how a project discovers its flaw eighteen months late.

HotOS · HotStorage · HotNets · HotInfra · HotCloudPerf · APSys

Stage 2 Royal Flush Full House

The full paper, and the poster that goes with it - the result the project is actually for. This is the only stage with grades, because it is the only one where the same finished paper has a choice of table:

Royal Flush - the best hand in the deck and the rarest. Single-digit-to-twenty percent acceptance, recognised by acronym alone.

Full House - a strong hand that wins most tables. Specialised venues with real review and a more focused audience, often where the more interesting arguments happen.

Royal Flush  ·  OSDI · SOSP · NSDI · FAST · SIGCOMM · SIGMOD · VLDB · CCS · S&P · ICSE

Full House  ·  NDSS · DSN · ICDCS · IMC · INFOCOM · IPDPS · SoCC · MLSys · SYSTOR · MSST

Stage 3 Looking Glass

The reflected version: longer, fuller, archival. Journals have no deadline, no cycle and no countdown - you submit when the work is ready and the clock never applies, which is why White Rabbit shows them as rolling submission and never counts them down. It is the last stage precisely because it is the one the calendar cannot rush.

What this is not

A ranking of research quality, and not a rule. Plenty of good work skips a stage, and plenty of the best ideas in systems appeared first as a Rabbit Hole workshop paper and never went further. Read it as the route most projects benefit from, not a ladder you owe anyone.

Stage 2's two grades track how a venue behaves - selectivity, breadth of audience - not how good any particular paper in it is. It is one judgement call recorded in one field of conferences.yml; if a venue looks misplaced, that is a one-line change and a reasonable pull request.

Ranks live in conferences.yml as tier: - rabbit-hole, royal-flush, full-house, looking-glass.
